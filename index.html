AIM: AI for Manufacturing

Reliable Decision-Making in High-Stakes Industrial Systems

NeurIPS 2026 Workshop · Sydney · Date TBA · Venue TBA

Manufacturing is one of the largest sectors of the global economy and a critical driver of technological innovation. From semiconductor fabrication and pharmaceutical production to factory operations and global supply chains, modern manufacturing systems generate vast amounts of data and present rich opportunities for AI-driven decision-making.

Yet manufacturing remains fundamentally different from many traditional AI application domains. Industrial systems operate under strict physical constraints, stringent safety requirements, limited opportunities for experimentation, and high costs of failure. As a result, successful AI solutions must be reliable, interpretable, data-efficient, and grounded in the realities of physical systems.

The AIM (AI for Manufacturing) workshop brings together researchers and practitioners from machine learning, optimization, control, operations research, robotics, and manufacturing to advance AI methods for high-stakes industrial systems. We seek to bridge the gap between advances in AI/ML and their deployment in real-world manufacturing environments.

Through invited talks, panels, contributed presentations, and posters, AIM aims to foster a community around principled, trustworthy, and scalable AI for manufacturing and the physical world.

The workshop aims to bring together researchers and practitioners across AI, optimization, control, operations research, and manufacturing. We invite submissions on AI methods and applications for manufacturing and other high-stakes industrial systems, with an emphasis on reliability, interpretability, and deployment under physical and operational constraints. For instance, we welcome work spanning the following application domains:

Process Optimization and Yield Enhancement

Process control, yield improvement, scientific machine learning, autonomous experimentation, and multi-fidelity optimization.

Manufacturing Systems

Production planning, scheduling, maintenance, inspection, resource allocation, and intelligent factory operations.

Causal Diagnostics and Root Cause Analysis

Causal discovery, fault diagnosis, explainable AI, counterfactual reasoning, and industrial monitoring.

Supply Chains and Logistics

Forecasting, inventory optimization, logistics, supply chain resilience, and decision-making under uncertainty.

Methodological topics include reinforcement learning and sequential decision-making, Bayesian and simulation-based optimization, causal inference, scientific machine learning, digital twins, foundation models for industrial data, learning-augmented algorithms, and other approaches that enable reliable, physically grounded, and deployable AI systems for manufacturing.

We particularly encourage submissions that combine methodological innovation with real-world impact, including interdisciplinary collaborations between academia and industry.

Submission Guidelines

  • Format: Single PDF following NeurIPS 2026 formatting guidelines. We anticipate a short-paper format for emerging ideas and preliminary results; final page limits will be posted here.
  • Review process: Double-blind review via OpenReview. Each submission receives at least two reviews. Authors and reviewers remain anonymous throughout.
  • Reviewer volunteering: We expect at least one author per submission to review two other papers, subject to expertise and conflict-of-interest constraints.
  • Presentation: Accepted papers will be presented as contributed talks or posters, depending on the number and quality of submissions.
